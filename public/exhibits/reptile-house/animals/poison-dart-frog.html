Poison Dart Frog

Description

Poison dart frogs are tiny amphibians that reside in humid environments like the rainforests of Central America and South America. They measure to just 0.75 to 1.5 inches long and weighing between 2 and 8.5 grams. They rely on rainforest moisture and water trapped inside plant leaves rather than large waterways, spending most of their days using their sharp eyesight and sticky tongues to hunt small insects like ants, mites, and termites.

Conservation

In the wild, poison dart frogs face natural predation from animals such as the fire-bellied snake, though they can live for 12 to 20 years and reach sexual maturity within 10 to 12 months. They reproduce sexually by laying clutches of 2 to 10 eggs, and overall, they are currently classified as a species of Least Concern.

Behavior

Unlike many frogs, poison dart frogs are terrestrial beings instead of being fully aquatic. Due to the abundant moisture and high humidity of the rainforest, they are able to live without access to a large waterway, finding the water they need for reproduction inside of plant leaves. Their small stature and the proportional size of their prey cause these frogs to be out hunting for food the majority of the day. These frogs have excellent eyesight to spot tiny insect prey hidden in the foliage, and then use their sticky tongues to reach out and snatch prey.
